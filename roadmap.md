# Lifelong Learning Roadmap — Raka

**Profile:** Indonesian beginner · 10–20 h/week · goal: build up until mastery ("the best").
**Priority order (your choice):** Math → Bio → Physics → then Software Engineering → then Business/Econ/Finance → Languages (English + Chinese).
**Mindset:** lifelong, compounding. Phases have *suggested* durations, not deadlines. A phase is done when you pass its **exit test**, not when the clock says so.

---

## How to use this

1. **Work one phase at a time.** Don't stack 6 domains.
2. Each phase = **foundation → build → exit test**.
3. **Exit test** is a real benchmark, not "I feel done." No pass = stay.
4. Weekly loop (10–20 h):
   - **4–6 h** new material (read/watch/lecture)
   - **6–10 h** problem-solving / practice / projects (this is where learning happens)
   - **1–2 h** spaced review of earlier phases (Anki)
   - **1 h** write 3 sentences: what I learned, what I'm stuck on, next step
5. Track: one notebook or app. Log hours + one win per week. Streak > volume.

## PHASE 1 — Math foundations
**Duration:** ~4–6 months · **Target:** comfort with pre-calc + algebra + basic trig + logic

**Modules (in order):**
1. Number systems & arithmetic fluency (fractions, exponents, radicals)
2. Algebra (linear equations, quadratics, functions, systems)
3. Graphs, functions, inequalities
4. Trigonometry basics (unit circle, identities)
5. Logic & proof by example (introduction to "why" not just "how")
6. Basic statistics (mean, variance, distributions, correlation)

**Resources (free):**
- Khan Academy Math (Algebra 1 → Trigonometry → Statistics & Probability) — structured, adaptive
- 3Blue1Brown *Essence of Algebra / Linear Algebra / Calculus* (YouTube) — intuition
- "Mathematics for Computer Science" notes (MIT OCW) for logic/proof — read when ready

**Exit test:** Solve ~40 mixed Khan problems at ≥90% without hints; hand-write a 1-page summary of each module; pass a logic/proof micro-quiz.

---

## PHASE 2 — Biology
**Duration:** ~3–4 months

**Modules:**
1. Molecules of life (atoms, bonds, water, pH, biomolecules)
2. Cells (structures, transport, membranes)
3. Genetics & DNA (inheritance, central dogma)
4. Biochemistry fundamentals (enzymes, metabolism, ATP)
5. Evolution & ecology basics

**Resources:**
- Khan Academy Biology (free, complete)
- "Molecular Biology of the Cell" (free online editions) for reference — optional depth
- CrashCourse Biology (YouTube) — fast review layer

**Exit test:** Explain central dogma + mitosis/meiosis + a metabolic pathway from memory; label a cell & a DNA strand; quiz ≥85%.

> Pair with Phase 1 math (stat genetics) — do 20 min of Khan bio each day alongside math, not stacked.

---

## PHASE 3 — Physics
**Duration:** ~4–5 months (needs the math from Phase 1)

**Modules:**
1. Mechanics (kinematics, forces, energy, momentum)
2. Waves & sound
3. Electricity & magnetism basics
4. Intro thermodynamics
5. (Stretch) Intro relativity / modern physics concepts

**Resources:**
- Khan Academy Physics (Mechanics → Electricity & Magnetism → Thermodynamics)
- 3Blue1Brown / Walter Lewin lectures for depth
- "Feynman Lectures on Physics" — reference for curious moments (not a first read)

**Exit test:** Solve mechanics + E&M problems set (≥80%); derive and explain 3 core equations (e.g., F=ma, conservation of energy, Ohm's law) from scratch.

---

## PHASE 4 — Software Engineering
**Duration:** ~6–9 months (this is a long one — it's a craft)

**Modules (in order, don't skip):**
1. **Programming fundamentals:** variables, control flow, functions — pick **Python** (fastest to read other languages)
2. **Data structures & algorithms** (Big-O, lists, dicts, trees, graphs, sorting, recursion)
3. **Core CS:** how computers work, memory, OS basics, networking, databases
4. **A language for the job:** Python for scripting/data, then **one "real" language** (Go or TypeScript — pick Go for systems clarity, TS if web)
5. **Version control (git), testing, basic DevOps**
6. **Build 3 projects** (small → medium → one with real users)

**Resources:**
- CS50 (Harvard, free) — best entry into CS
- freeCodeCamp / The Odin Project — project-driven
- "Grokking Algorithms" (book) — DSA visual
- "Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs" — optional depth
- LeetCode — practice DSA (after fundamentals, not before)

**Exit test:** Build and deploy a real project; pass a timed DSA quiz (5 medium problems); explain how your program uses memory & what O(n log n) means in your own words.

> This phase is where you start **earning** (freelance, junior roles, or building products). Treat "earn first real money" as the exit signal.

---

## PHASE 5 — Business, Economics & Finance
**Duration:** ~3–4 months, can overlap Phase 4

**Modules:**
1. Microeconomics (supply/demand, incentives)
2. Macroeconomics (inflation, money, growth)
3. Personal finance (compounding, budgeting, investing basics, risk)
4. Accounting & corporate finance (read financial statements)
5. Business fundamentals (value creation, markets, unit economics)

**Resources:**
- "Microeconomics in One Lesson" (Ludwig von Mises) + Krugman intro
- Khan Academy Economics
- "The Simplest Things in Finance" / Investopedia for the finance block
- "The Psychology of Money" (book) — mindset layer

**Exit test:** Read and interpret a real company's P&L + balance sheet; build a personal net-worth + compounding model; explain one market mechanism from first principles.

## PHASE 6 — Languages: English (primary) + Chinese
**Duration:** ongoing (this one *never* ends)

**English (you're Indonesian — high leverage):**
- **Input:** read 1 book/week; watch English content (the CS50, 3Blue1Brown above are in English — you're already consuming it)
- **Output:** write your weekly 3-sentence log in English; do mock conversations
- **Goal:** read a technical paper & write it up in English.

**Chinese (Mandarin — hardest, so start slow & persistent):**
- HSK 1 → 2 → 3 (vocabulary ladder)
- Textbook/Anki deck + one 30-min listening input daily
- 5+ years realistic to real fluency. Daily habit > weekly sprint.

**Exit test:** English — pass a reading comprehension at technical level + 10-min recorded monologue. Chinese — HSK 2 minimum at month 12 of this phase.

## PHASE 7 — Personal Development
Not a "phase you finish" — a habit stack:
- **Health:** 30 min movement/day, 7–8h sleep, protein. (You can't learn on zero sleep.)
- **Mind:** 10 min daily mindfulness/journaling; read on decision-making & bias
- **Skill compounds:** the weekly 3-sentence log is the engine of this whole system
- Read on thinking: "Thinking, Fast and Slow" (Kahneman), "The Art of Learning"

---

## The compounding loop

After **each** phase's exit test, you do **not** move on and forget. You:
1. Keep **2 h/week** on spaced review of everything before (Anki).
2. Each new phase builds on the last: math→bio→physics→software→business→language.
3. **Every 12 months** do a "meta-review": what's decaying? bump the Anki deck; what's compounding? go deeper there.
4. Specialization = pick **one** domain to push from "capable" to "top 1%". For a lifelong 'best in history' ambition, the most-leveraged single peak for an Indonesian self-learner is **Software Engineering + Data/AI** (earn, build, global reach) — but only after the science foundation. That's why science-first is the right call: you'll understand the CS deeper than peers who skipped physics.
